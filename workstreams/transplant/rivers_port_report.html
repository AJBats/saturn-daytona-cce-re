Rivers of Data — what got ported, what didn't

Reconciling the driving-model reverse engineering (data_flow_chains.md “Stream to Ocean” + transplant_manifest.md) against the actual port state, the funcfinder closure, and the car-struct seam audit.

sources: SaturnReverseTest/workstreams/driving_model · DaytonaCCEReverse/workstreams/transplant · coverage_audit 2026-07-04 · car_struct_audit 2026-07-04

The research was right, and it was never fully executed. The manifest chose “Option 3 — bring DUSA physics + AI + track data + collision as a complete package,” and it explicitly marked the track/surface/segment functions SHARED. The port shipped section 1a only — the 18-call player pipeline — and “porting complete” got attached to that slice. The car didn’t move because Chain 4’s springs (13.1 KB of track tables) and their shared readers were documented as inputs but never carried as cargo.

Everything since (seam audit, subsystem stamping, gap closure) has been re-deriving manifest sections 1b/1d from bytes — with funcfinder rigor the skeleton manifest never had. That’s why it feels non-mechanical: we’re not off the map, we’re surveying the parts of the map that were sketched but never walked.

What the research got wrong — the honest list

  1. “All gaps CLOSED” was a comprehension claim, not a port manifest.

    It meant “we understand how position/heading are computed.” The springs’ producers (shared code, track tables) sat outside that scope by design — correct as documentation, fatal as a shipping list.

  2. The dispatcher was a Ghidra ghost.

    Manifest names FUN_0602EEB8 as the pipeline dispatcher; it’s a mid-prologue artifact. The true external entry is 0x0602ECF2 (found during the port; corrected in the audit tooling).

  3. Execution stopped at §1a and nobody flagged it.

    Option 3 said “complete package”; Steps 1–11 built up the player pipeline only, and “porting complete” got declared at the §1a boundary. The manifest’s own SHARED markers were the warning that went unread.

  4. The byte-closure keeps finding structure the manifest never saw.

    The 0601D5F4 tail-call dispatcher cluster (“unknown game system”), sibling handlers 06008730/060086C0, the 06026xxx cluster, hidden thunk 06030EFC, tail-call target 06036068. Skeleton status (“sizes TBD, PENDING”) was real — it was never funcfinder-verified.

  5. The 20fps→30fps ×2/3 scaling catalog is dead weight.

    Not wrong — superseded. The embedded-DUSA design runs the model at its native 20 Hz tick, so per-frame constants stay untouched. Ignore that section when porting.

  6. “Collision is emergent” is still a hypothesis.

    Plausible, empirically motivated — and untestable until AI cars + shared track system + track data all exist on the CCE side.
